# Bad-strategy diagnostics

Rumelt identifies four hallmarks of bad strategy. Use the tests below on any draft, including your own.

## 1. Fluff
Restating the obvious in inflated language ("customer-centric digital transformation leveraging synergies").
- **Test:** Replace each abstract phrase with what someone would actually do differently on Monday. If nothing changes, it is fluff.
- **Fix:** Rewrite as a concrete choice, or delete.

## 2. Failure to face the challenge
The document never names the obstacle, so it cannot be evaluated.
- **Test:** Can a reader state, in one sentence, what problem the strategy solves? Ask three readers; if their answers differ, the diagnosis is missing.
- **Fix:** Write the diagnosis first and check every action against it.

## 3. Mistaking goals for strategy
"Grow revenue 30%" or "become the market leader" are desires, not plans for overcoming obstacles.
- **Test:** Does the text explain *how* the goal will be reached against the stated obstacles, and what is given up?
- **Fix:** Keep the goal as an outcome measure; build the guiding policy that would make it reachable.

## 4. Bad strategic objectives
Either a "dog's dinner" (a long list of unrelated initiatives) or "blue sky" objectives that restate the challenge without a path.
- **Test:** Count objectives. More than about seven usually signals no prioritisation. Check whether each objective is attainable with known resources and is a step toward overcoming the crux.
- **Fix:** Choose proximate objectives — near enough that the organisation can act on them now.

## Additional checks
- **Exclusion test:** list three things a competitor could reasonably do that this strategy rules out for us. If none, the policy is too broad.
- **Resource test:** each action names what is stopped or reduced to fund it.
- **Coherence test:** at least some actions should reinforce each other; a set of independent initiatives is a portfolio, not a strategy.
- **Template-strategy warning:** vision / mission / values / goals documents with no diagnosis are a common format; they can coexist with strategy but do not replace it.
