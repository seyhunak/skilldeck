# Willingness-to-pay research methods

Each method has a typical use and known biases. Combine at least two where possible.

## Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter
Four questions per respondent: at what price is the product too cheap (quality doubt), a bargain, getting expensive, and too expensive. Plotting cumulative curves gives an acceptable price range.
- **Use:** early-stage range finding for a well-understood product.
- **Bias:** respondents anchor on current prices; no purchase commitment; does not test packaging.

## Gabor-Granger
Ask purchase likelihood at a sequence of price points; derive a demand curve and revenue-maximising price.
- **Use:** single product with a clear concept.
- **Bias:** overstates purchase intent; sequence order affects results.

## Conjoint / discrete choice analysis
Respondents choose between bundles that vary features and price; statistical models estimate the value of each attribute.
- **Use:** packaging decisions and feature trade-offs.
- **Bias:** requires careful design, adequate sample sizes, and specialist analysis; still hypothetical.

## MaxDiff
Respondents pick most and least important features from sets.
- **Use:** ranking features to decide what goes in which tier; does not give prices directly.

## Qualitative value interviews
Explore economic value, budget owner, alternatives, and the purchase process. Ask about past purchases rather than hypothetical intent.
- **Use:** B2B, low sample sizes, uncovering value metric candidates.

## Behavioural evidence (strongest)
- Live price tests on new traffic or new leads.
- Win/loss analysis with price as a stated or observed factor.
- Discount levels needed to close deals by segment.
- Upgrade triggers: which limits drive customers to the next tier.

## Reporting rule
Always report the method, sample, segment, and date with any WTP number, and describe it as evidence of a range rather than a precise price.
