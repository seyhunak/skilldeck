# Quarterly Business Review Routine

## Objective
Produce, every quarter, an honest picture of business performance against plan and objectives, and convert it into a small number of explicit decisions: what to continue, stop, start, or re-resource next quarter. The outputs are a reconciled metrics pack, a short pre-read, a focused review meeting, and an action and decision log that the next review checks. This routine is for internal leadership or business-unit reviews; customer-facing QBRs with clients need a different, customer-value-focused agenda.

## Trigger
Run once per quarter, starting about ten working days before the quarter-end review date (typically in the first two to three weeks after quarter close, once financial figures are available). A person — usually a chief of staff, strategy lead, or business operations manager — owns the routine. It is guidance for people; it does not run on its own, though an assistant can help draft each step.

## Prerequisites
- Agreed metric definitions and a KPI tree or metric list that has not changed silently since last quarter.
- Access to the plan or budget for the quarter, the current OKRs, and the previous QBR's action log.
- Data owners identified for each metric (finance, sales operations, product analytics, customer success).
- A review meeting booked with the decision-makers, and a deadline for the pre-read.

## Inputs
- Quarter actuals for KPIs and financials, with plan and prior-quarter and prior-year comparatives.
- OKR scores and confidence ratings from each team.
- Functional leaders' short written updates (one page maximum each): results, issues, requests.
- Previous QBR decisions and actions with status.
- Material external developments (competitor moves, regulation, macro events) noted by leaders.

## Steps
1. **Collect inputs (day -10 to -7).** Send a request to data owners and functional leaders with the template and deadline. Ask for numbers in the agreed definitions and for written explanations of any variance beyond the agreed materiality threshold.
2. **Reconcile the metric pack (day -7 to -5).** Combine figures into one table. Check totals against finance, confirm definitions match last quarter, and log every discrepancy with its owner. Do not publish numbers that do not reconcile; mark them "provisional".
3. **Analyse performance (day -5 to -4).** Compute variances versus plan, prior quarter, and prior year. Walk down the KPI tree (using `kpi-tree-design` if no tree exists) to locate which drivers explain movements in top-line metrics. Separate timing effects from structural changes. Review OKR scores for patterns: systematic over-achievement may signal sandbagging; systematic misses may signal over-commitment or a broken assumption.
4. **Review last quarter's actions.** Mark each decision and action as done, in progress, slipped, or dropped, with reasons. Unfinished actions without a reason are themselves a finding.
5. **Identify decisions needed (day -4).** From the analysis, draft three to five decision items: re-prioritising investment, changing a target, stopping an initiative, addressing a risk. For each, prepare a short decision note with options and a recommendation using `decision-memo-writing`.
6. **Write and circulate the pre-read (day -3).** Maximum about four pages plus appendices: summary, KPI table, key variances and drivers, OKR status, action-log review, decisions requested. Circulate at least two working days before the meeting and ask attendees to read it in advance; questions can be collected before the meeting.
7. **Run the meeting (day 0).** Suggested agenda for a 2-3 hour session: 10 minutes for silent pre-read catch-up if needed; 20 minutes for performance questions (no slide re-presentation); the bulk of the time on decision items, one at a time, ending each with an explicit decision, owner, and date; 15 minutes on risks and external changes; 10 minutes to confirm the action log.
8. **Publish outcomes (day +1 to +2).** Circulate decisions, actions with owners and dates, changed targets, and any OKR adjustments to attendees and the teams affected, after the meeting chair approves the summary.
9. **Feed the next cycle.** Update the action log, record lessons about the process itself (late data, unclear definitions), and adjust the template for next quarter.

## Decision points
- If a material metric cannot be reconciled by day -5, publish it as provisional with a range, and add the data issue to the agenda rather than delaying the review.
- If the quarter shows a structural break against plan (for example a sustained shortfall in a core driver), schedule a separate re-planning session rather than trying to rebuild the plan in the QBR.
- If more than five decisions are proposed, the chair prioritises; remaining items move to regular leadership meetings.
- If a decision item lacks a credible option analysis, defer it with a clear owner and date instead of deciding on opinion.
- If OKRs are consistently scored at full achievement, revisit target-setting conventions in the next planning cycle.

## Expected output
- Reconciled KPI and financial pack with a discrepancy log.
- Pre-read document (summary, KPI table, variance drivers, OKR status, action review, decisions requested).
- Meeting decision record: each decision, rationale, owner, date.
- Updated action log carried into next quarter.
- Short process retrospective note.

## Validation checklist
- [ ] Every KPI uses the same definition as last quarter, or the change is stated in the pack.
- [ ] All figures in the pre-read reconcile to finance or are clearly marked provisional.
- [ ] Each material variance has an explanation from its owner, not an analyst's guess.
- [ ] Every previous action has a status and reason.
- [ ] Each decision item has options, a recommendation, and a named decision-maker.
- [ ] The pre-read was circulated at least two working days before the meeting.
- [ ] Every meeting decision has an owner and a due date in the published log.

## Human approval
The leadership team, chaired by the CEO or business-unit head, makes all decisions; an assistant may draft analyses, notes, and the pre-read but must not present drafts as decisions. Data owners approve their figures before circulation. The chair approves the outcome summary before it is distributed beyond attendees. Any change to company-level targets or budgets follows the organisation's formal approval process, and information shared beyond the leadership team must be cleared for confidentiality.
