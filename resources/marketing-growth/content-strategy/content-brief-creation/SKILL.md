# Content Brief Creation

A procedure for producing a brief that lets a writer deliver a publishable first draft without guessing. A good brief decides who the piece is for, what it must add beyond what already ranks, and how the editor will judge it.

## When to use

- Commissioning a new article, guide, comparison page, or glossary entry.
- Refreshing a decaying page and the writer needs direction.
- Scaling content production with freelancers who lack product context.

Not for deciding which topics to pursue (do keyword research and strategy first) or for producing the article itself.

## Inputs

- **Target query and cluster** — primary query, supporting queries, estimated demand. Required.
- **Business purpose** — the action the piece should lead to and where it sits in the funnel. Required.
- **Audience** — segment, expertise level, and the job they are doing when they search. Required.
- **Top-ranking results** — URLs or notes on the current top results. Recommended.
- **Subject-matter input** — product facts, internal data, SME availability. Recommended.
- **Style constraints** — voice guide, word-count range, formatting conventions.

## Procedure

1. **Define the reader and their moment.** One or two sentences: who is searching, what they already know, what triggered the search, and what they need to walk away with. This shapes depth and vocabulary more than any keyword.
2. **Analyze the results page.** For the top results, record format (guide, list, template, tool), approximate depth, the subtopics they share (the baseline the piece must cover), what they miss or do poorly (outdated information, no examples, no visuals, generic advice), and SERP features (questions box, video, images). Shared subtopics are table stakes; gaps are the opportunity.
3. **Choose the angle.** State what will make this piece better than the existing results for this reader: original data, a worked example, a template, expert input, a clearer framework, or a narrower audience focus. A brief without an angle produces a remix of the top results.
4. **List must-cover subtopics.** Combine table-stakes subtopics, supporting queries, and "people also ask" questions. Mark each as required or optional, and note subtopics to deliberately exclude because they belong to another page (preventing cannibalization).
5. **Draft the outline.** H1 and H2/H3 structure with one line per section on what it must accomplish. Put the most direct answer to the query near the top. Indicate where examples, visuals, tables, or templates belong.
6. **Specify sources and evidence.** List primary sources to consult (official documentation, standards, original research, internal data) and where claims need citations. Instruct the writer to verify every statistic at the original source and to avoid citing competitor blogs as authority. Do not put unverified numbers into the brief.
7. **Specify SEO requirements.** Recommended title and meta description direction, H1, primary and supporting queries to cover naturally, internal links to include (with suggested anchors and target URLs) and pages that should link to the new piece, image alt-text expectations, and structured data if relevant.
8. **Set the call to action.** The next step for the reader, matched to funnel stage (a template download for awareness, a demo for decision).
9. **Write acceptance criteria.** Checklist the editor will use: answers the query in the first section, covers all required subtopics, angle delivered, sources cited at primary level, internal links present, voice guide followed, word count within range, no unsupported claims.
10. **Add logistics.** Word-count range (based on what the topic requires, not on competitor length alone), deadline, SME contact, review steps.

## Expected output

```markdown
# Content Brief: <working title>
**Primary query:** ... · **Supporting:** ... · **Intent:** ... · **Funnel stage:** ... · **Target URL:** ...

## Reader and moment
## Angle (why this beats current results)
## SERP notes
| Result | Format | Covers | Misses |
## Must-cover / optional / exclude
## Outline
## Sources and evidence requirements
## SEO requirements (title direction, meta, links in/out, schema)
## Call to action
## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] ...
## Logistics (length, deadline, SME, reviewers)
```

## Constraints and limitations

- The brief does not supply facts; writers must verify all claims at primary sources.
- SERP snapshots change; re-check before publishing if the brief is weeks old.
- Word counts are guides; padding to reach a number reduces quality.
- Topics in health, finance, legal, or safety need a qualified reviewer named in the brief.

## Examples

Primary query: "how to calculate customer lifetime value". Reader: a startup marketer who knows CAC but not cohort maths. SERP: top results give the simple formula (average order value × purchase frequency × lifespan) but none show how to handle churn-based subscription maths or include a worked spreadsheet. Angle: two methods (simple and churn-based) with a worked example and a downloadable sheet. Exclude: "LTV to CAC ratio benchmarks" (separate planned page; link to it). Acceptance: formulas defined with every variable, worked example adds up, one internal link to the unit-economics guide.
